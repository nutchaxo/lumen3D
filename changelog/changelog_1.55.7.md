# Changelog v1.55.7 (Web Platform)

## [CHANGED]

- **The Z-stack browser lays the stack flat the moment it opens, gently**: opening the browser now poses the volume top-down at once — travelling there over a second and a half along the shortest arc, eased at both ends — instead of snapping only when the cursor first entered the track, on a specimen already reduced to one slice. The pose is the sample's upper face toward the camera, as seen from above the microscope, spun in-plane by the smallest amount from wherever the volume was: `VolumeViewer.setView('xy', { side: 'top', spin: 'nearest', animate: 1500 })`. The 3D notch then leaves the rotation free; the track lays the stack flat again (nearest spin, 1.2 s) and locks it. A rotate drag cancels a pose in flight; a restored workspace keeps its saved pose (no opening move).
- **A slider turns the slices on screen**: the new *Rotation* control of the browser (0–360°) starts on the spin the opening pose landed on, not on 0, and turns the slices in-plane at once, through the rotation lock. Its angle counts from the calibrated frame's own spin, travels to the sibling Compare panels in `SYNC_ZSTACK_SLICE` (camera sync is blocked while the track holds the view) and is saved in the workspace (`spin`). `zstack-browser` 1.2.0, `platformCompat >=1.55.7` (an older core would ignore side `top` and the animation).
- **`VolumeViewer.setView` gains `side: 'top' | 'bottom'`, `spin: 'frame' | 'nearest' | degrees`, `animate` and `force`, and reports the spin it landed on** (`{ spinDeg }`): `'top'` / `'bottom'` are resolved from the dataset's sample side; `'nearest'` picks, among the poses Rz(θ)·Q₀ of the same face, the one maximising ⟨Rz(θ), Q·Q₀⁻¹⟩ — the smallest rotation that lays the slices flat; a number is the slider. `_poseTo` / `_stepPoseAnimation` run the slerp in the render loop with one camera notification when it settles. The default (`spin: 'frame'`, no animation) is the former behaviour, bit for bit.

## [ADDED]

- **Sample side — "right side up" / "upside down" — in the admin dataset editor**: a confocal stack exported from Imaris shows the sample from below (its +Z face is the underside, imaged from the objective of an inverted microscope), so the default 3D view and the z-stack looked at it upside down. The new *Sample side* switch of the 3D orientation section writes `metadata.upsideDown` (a boolean, always posted so a merge backend keeps an explicit `false`) and turns the preview over on the spot (`SET_SAMPLE_UPSIDE_DOWN` → `VolumeViewer.setSampleUpsideDown(flag, { turnOver: true })`): the operator picks the side by looking, then calibrates. Other teams whose files are the right way up leave it off — the default.
- **What the flag does in the viewer**: the raw pose of an upside-down file is a half-turn about its X axis (`getRawPoseQuaternion`: the initial pose, *reset view*, the `3d` view), so the sample is shown as seen from above; the z-stack browser looks at the top face (`side: 'top'` = the −Z face when upside down, +Z otherwise) instead of the −Z face hard-coded in v1.54.2. The calibration (`metadata.orientation`, a cube pose) carries the half-turn like any other rotation: when the switch flips on the preview, the core turns the volume over with its home pose and frame, the orientation plugin turns its Q_base over on the same message and hands it back (`SAMPLE_SIDE_RESULT`), and the panel stores it — the saved frame is the one the operator sees; a saved default view (a statement about anatomy, Q_anat) keeps meaning the same thing. `orientation-axes` 1.3.0, `platformCompat >=1.55.7`.
