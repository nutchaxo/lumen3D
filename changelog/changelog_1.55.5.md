# Changelog v1.55.5 (Web Platform)

## [OPTIMIZED]

- **A cut across the stack downloads its bricks, not whole packs**: the Studio's native pass fetched every pack a brick of the plane lived in, whole. A brick pack holds 128 bricks in z-layer order, so a plane along the stack (XY) needs most of its packs, but a cut across it needs a few bricks of many packs: on the reference 3789² dataset an XZ cut wanted 21 MB of tiles out of 157 MB of packs, a YZ cut 24 MB out of 572 MB, an oblique cut 256 MB out of about 750 MB. `BrickLoader.loadBrickTasks` gains `byteRanges`: for the packs a batch needs only part of, the bricks' byte intervals are merged across gaps under 512 KB and fetched with one `Range` request per run (a pack whose runs still cover 60 % of it, or would need more than 48 requests, is fetched whole, so an XY cut is unchanged). A server answering 200 to a `Range` request has sent the whole pack, which is kept as such; any other answer drops the plan for that pack and fetches it whole, so a host without range support loses nothing. Apache serves byte ranges natively; the Python dev server now answers them too (`AdminHandler._serve_static_range`, 206 + `Content-Range`, everything unusual left to the ordinary 200). The 3D viewer's level streams are untouched. Measured locally: the XZ cut moved from 157 MB to 17 MB in 12 requests.

## [ADDED]

- **The Studio's picture climbs the levels instead of waiting for native**: the upgrade behind *Open in Studio* now runs in stages — every brick level finer than the one the preview came from, coarse to fine, native last (on the reference dataset from a 512 preview: 1024 for 13 MB, 2048 for 51 MB, native for 195 MB). Each stage is a complete picture of the same frame, the previous one standing in wherever a chunk is still on its way, so a usable 2048² picture arrives after a quarter of the native download and *Stop here* (the former *Cancel*) keeps the best complete picture so far. A coarser stage is kept only when it costs no more than 30 % of the next finer one (a cut across the stack, where the coarser levels are not cheaper, goes straight to native), and once the rate measured on the stages already done says native is less than 15 s away the remaining intermediate stages are skipped — on a fast link the ladder costs almost nothing, on a slow host it is what shows progress.
- **Bytes and time left in the Studio's progress bar**: the label now reads `1024 × 1024: 41/64 chunks · 8.3/13 MB · ~6 s left` (`studio.loadingStage`, `studio.etaSeconds`, `studio.etaMinutes` in the four languages; the estimate appears after two seconds and three percent of the bytes), so the wait is a known quantity rather than a mystery. The compressed size of every brick comes from the pack index (`BrickLoader.taskBytes` / `estimateTaskBytes`).

## [CHANGED]

- **`_renderNativeSliceForStudio(options)`** takes `lod` (the brick level to render, 0 = native) and `renderRes` (the frame to render in; every stage of an upgrade shares the native frame, so partial pictures swap in place), reports `bytesDone` / `bytesTotal` / `etaSeconds` to `onProgress`, and its result carries `lod`, `bytesTotal` and `elapsedMs`; `_nativeSliceBricksForSpec(spec, dims, lod)` picks bricks at any level. Stage selection: `_studioStages`.
- **What this does not change**: a cut along the stack (XY) still has to bring in its whole brick layer — every 64³ tile carries 64 slices of which the cut uses one to three — because the pack format stores bricks, not slices. Making an XY cut cost a few megabytes needs a slice-major layout of the native level in the preprocessing pipeline; that is a pipeline change, not a viewer one.
- **Tests**: `tests/js/test_brick_loader_ranges.mjs` (plan, one request per run, 200 and 416 fallbacks, default untouched), `tests/js/test_studio_lod_ladder.mjs` (stage selection from the viewer source), `tests/test_static_range.py` (the dev server's 206 answers and its 200 fallbacks).
