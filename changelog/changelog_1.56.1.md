# Changelog v1.56.1 (Web Platform)

## [FIXED]

- **Studio figures of a stabilised timelapse show what the screen shows**: a `live` dataset whose registration is applied to the volume is drawn stabilised (the ray-marcher samples each timepoint through `volumeWarp`, in a display box larger than the acquisition box), but the slicer ignored it, so every Studio figure of such a series — slice tool, Z-stack browser, native upgrade, Compare Studio — was the raw acquisition plane, shifted and turned by that timepoint's rigid transform and, for the z-stack, taken from another depth. The slicer now samples through the same warp (`VOLUME_WARP`, `volumeWarp` linked from the volume material, in-box test in texture space) in its colour and raw paths; a plane's value sweeps the display box (`VolumeSlicer.planeSweep/planeDepth`), the z-stack slab is the displayed one (`VolumeViewer.getClipSpace()`), and the frame grows to hold the display box (`VolumeSlicer.frameExtent`, 1.71 units instead of 1.5 on the lab's timelapse; the pixel size and render sizes follow). The native pass picks its bricks through the warp, with a per-brick run of voxels along the axis the warped normal leans on most. Measured in the browser at timepoint 25 (a 30–36° transform from the first frame), Z-stack slab, spin 20°: the Studio picture now matches the 3D view without any rotation or mirror, where the former figure only matched after a 208° turn plus a mirror, with a different set of cells. Unwarped datasets are unchanged, number for number.
- **Four-channel slabs keep their outside transparent in the Studio**: a projected slab (MIP, average) of a volume with four channels had no free byte to say where the volume is, so its recoloured picture was opaque over the whole crop — a black frame, and black corners around a turned z-stack slab, on the light Compare backdrop. The raw slab now carries a coverage mask read from the alpha of the colour render it is built next to (no extra render), shared by the native pictures of the same frame and honoured by `SliceCompositor` on the GPU (an R8 texture) and on the CPU. Volumes with fewer channels keep their coverage in channel 3.
- **Importing a Studio JSON keeps the figure recolourable and applies it**: the import replaced the document wholesale, so a Compare figure's cells lost their pixel buffers (channel edits did nothing) and the imported channel settings were never applied to the picture. The import now applies the file to the figure that is open: cells keep their buffers, geometry and calibration; the file's layers, guides and channel settings are applied, and the picture is recoloured. A file saved for another figure (another dataset or pair of datasets, another size, plane or timepoint) still brings its annotations but keeps the open figure's channels, with a warning. A malformed file changes nothing. The import is one undo step, and undo/redo now recolour the picture to the channels of the state they restore.
- **Compare Studio captures of a stabilised timelapse**: rendered with the frame's real extent, so they keep about one pixel per voxel.

Tests: `tests/js/test_slicer_warp.mjs` (every define combination, a Studio plane point reaches the same texture coordinate as the ray-marcher's chain, frame coverage of the display box, the z-stack slab, the warped brick picker against a brute-force sampling, unwarped results unchanged), `tests/js/test_slab_coverage_mask.mjs` (the mask follows the crop and reaches every native picture, never serialised), `tests/js/test_slice_compositor.mjs` (masked alpha on the GPU and CPU paths, texture budget), `tests/js/test_studio_json_import.mjs` (buffers re-attached, channels applied and recoloured, mismatching figures, malformed files, undo).
