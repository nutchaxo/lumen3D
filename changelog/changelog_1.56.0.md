# Changelog v1.56.0 (Web Platform)

## [ADDED]

- **Export the 3D view as a PNG, from the viewer itself**: a new button in the floating canvas controls (next to *Reset view*) opens a small panel — size (*Screen*, 2×, 4× or a custom width, the height following the view; clamped to what a browser can hold, 16384 px a side and 268 megapixels, and said so), background (*As displayed*, transparent, black, white) and a scale bar. The image is rendered again at the requested size, not upscaled: `VolumeViewer.renderViewImage()` draws the current scene through the same pre-render sync as the live loop, at the idle (full) step count, in tiles of at most 2048 px (`camera.setViewOffset`), each read back and assembled into one canvas; the ray-march dither now reads `gl_FragCoord + fragCoordOffset`, so a tile's pixels are exactly those a single full-size render would give and no seam shows. The scale bar is the 1-2-5 bar of the screen (`Utils.niceScaleLength`), its length recomputed for the exported pixel (µm per output px = µm per CSS px at the specimen depth ÷ the export scale). Progress is shown per tile with a Cancel button; the export waits while bricks stream and starts the image again if the volume or a display setting changes under it (at most three times). It is always there, whatever plugins a host installed; hidden in Compare panels and embedded previews, which have their own figure export. The marketplace *Screenshot* plugin is unchanged.
- **Annotations of the Studio can be rotated**: every layer has a rotation (degrees). Rectangles, ellipses and text turn about their centre (hit-testing, resize handles and the selection outline turn with them, and resizing a turned box keeps the opposite corner in place); lines, arrows, distances, angles and scale bars have the turn applied to their points, so what they measure does not move — a distance keeps its µm and an angle its degrees even with anisotropic pixels (both turned in physical space), and a scale bar keeps measuring exactly its value along its new direction (pixel length `value / hypot(ux·psx, uy·psy)`, value and unit edits keep the direction). Three ways to turn a layer: the *Rotation* slider, degree field and reset button of the properties panel; a round knob above the selected layer (Shift snaps to 15°); `[` / `]` (±15°, Shift ±1°). One undo step per gesture; locked layers refuse; a figure saved before this version opens unrotated.
- **`js/core/slice-compositor.js` (`SliceCompositor`)**: colours a slice's raw channel values with exactly the slicer's channel math (window, gamma, opacity, colour, sum) on its own small WebGL2 canvas, with a lookup-table fallback on the CPU; also gives the slice's own per-channel histograms. Loaded by `viewer.html` and `compare.html`.

## [FIXED]

- **The Studio figure of the Z-stack browser is oriented as the screen shows it**: since v1.55.7 the browser lays the stack flat with its top face toward the camera (the mirror image when the file is upside down) and turns it by the calibrated frame and the *Rotation* slider, but *Open in Studio* still drew the raw voxel plane (image right = voxel +X, seen from +Z) — rotated and, for an upside-down sample, mirrored against what was on screen. The Studio plane is now an oblique plane whose in-plane axes are the screen's own (`VolumeViewer.getScreenFrameInVolume()`, the settled pose when a turn is still in flight): yaw 0 on the +Z face, 180 on the −Z face (depth `1 − c` there), and the roll that best matches the screen's right and up. The preview, the native upgrade and the Compare Studio all use it. In the browser's free-rotation mode it is the flat pose nearest what is on screen. Measured in the browser, on a calibrated dataset at spins of 37° and 123° (the latter upside down): the best match between the Studio picture and the 3D view, searched over every angle and a mirror, is the identity (correlation 0.96 and 0.95), where the former figure matched only after a 236° turn plus a mirror.
- **The channel controls of the Studio work again**: the colour, min/max/gamma handles, opacity, on/off and solo of the Studio's channel panel did nothing since v1.55.3 — the slicer's `recompose` only re-rendered its own sources, not the Studio's preview and native pictures, whose LOD0 atlas is thrown away after the native pass. A Studio slice now carries its raw channel values (`raw`: the atlas texels themselves, from a raw-output variant of the slice shader, cropped to the Studio frame; the native pass fills channels it did not download from the preview, and a chunk that failed keeps the preview's values), and the Studio colours them with its own channel state through `SliceCompositor` — at native resolution, without a new render, without moving the frame (annotations keep their coordinates). A progressive native refresh never reverts an edit: it is coloured with the document's channels, not the viewer's. The Studio's histograms are those of the slice itself. The Compare Studio recolours each cell from its own panel's raw values.
- **Slab pictures (MIP, average) use one convention everywhere**: a slab is projected per channel on the raw values and then windowed and coloured (the usual convention for a multichannel projection, and the only one under which *average* is an average intensity); the slice stage, figure captures and the Studio now agree. Before, the colour path projected already-windowed colours, so an averaged slab came out brighter in one view than another and overlapping channel colours were mixed before the maximum.
- **Pixel size of Studio slices**: the slicer draws 1.5 cube units across the render and one cube unit is the longest physical axis in every direction, so a Studio pixel is square, `1.5 · maxP / renderRes`. The pixel size used the extent along each image axis instead, which was only right along the longest axis: vertical distances and angles measured in the Studio on XZ/YZ cuts, and on the short side of non-square XY cuts, were wrong by `p_axis / maxP`. They now read true (existing figures re-measure differently on those cuts).
- **Compare Studio: undo/redo and *Save JSON* no longer copy the panels' pixel buffers**, which froze the page or failed once the cells carried their raw values; a saved figure holds the layout and the layers only. Compare also initialised the Studio twice, so every shortcut acted twice (one Ctrl+Z undid two steps).
- **Studio**: the properties panel of a selected layer is translated (its labels stayed in English); a z-stack figure is stamped `XY` again; a Compare figure's scale bar, distance or angle takes its calibration from the cell its middle lies in, so turning it never hands it to a neighbouring panel's µm/px; the export no longer logs a warning for a missing native render; the scratch canvases of a closed Studio are released.

Tests: `tests/js/test_view_export.mjs`, `tests/js/test_view_export_consistency.mjs` (seamless tiles and dither, µm per output pixel against the on-screen bar, clamping, file name, restart on a volume or display change, state restored), `tests/js/test_studio_layer_rotation.mjs` (turned boxes, baked point layers, measurements kept with anisotropic pixels, knob, keys, undo steps, JSON, Compare cell calibration), `tests/js/test_zstack_studio_orientation.mjs` (the Studio plane's right/up equal the screen's for flat and oblique poses, both faces; square pixels), `tests/js/test_slice_compositor.mjs` (the shader's channel math, GPU and lookup-table paths, projected slabs), `tests/js/test_studio_raw_channels.mjs` (edits survive native refreshes, no re-crop, histograms), `tests/js/test_studio_compare_document.mjs` (history and JSON never carry pixel buffers).
